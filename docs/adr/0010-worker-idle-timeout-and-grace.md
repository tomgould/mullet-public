# 0010. A silent worker is stopped, and a finished one gets a grace window to exit

Status: Accepted

Context: A Claude worker that stalled held the machine-wide Claude slot until its wall clock ran out (3600 s by default), and a CLI that hung after printing its final result made the runner wait out the whole wall clock too. opencode already stopped on wall clock, 20 s after a fatal error, and past its spend, but had neither of these two stops.

Decision: Both executors watch their worker (`Watch` in `mullet/jobs/executors/base.py`). Every stream line and every byte written to stderr counts as output. A worker with no output for the idle limit has its process group killed, and the attempt is an abort and a provider error with the reason "stopped: no output for N minutes", so it does not count against the worker, like the spend and provider stops. The idle limit is `jobs.idle_minutes`; when that is unset it is the larger of 15 minutes and 1.5 times the profile's longest check or test timeout, because a worker running the slowest check itself prints nothing until it ends. Once a worker has given its final reply (Claude's `result` event; opencode's last step, one that ends with `stop`, or with text and no tool calls when opencode gives no reason), it has `jobs.grace_seconds` to exit, at least 5 seconds (`MIN_GRACE_SECONDS`), because Claude Code saves its session file after the final reply and a resumed attempt needs it. After that its process group is killed and the attempt counts as finished: the gates judge the work, as they do after a timeout. The runner reads both settings once per step and passes them on in the attempt's `Budget`.

Consequences: A hung CLI costs at most the grace window after it finishes, and a stalled one the idle limit, not the wall clock. With the default profile (a 3600 s test timeout) the derived idle limit is 90 minutes, longer than the default wall clock, so only a profile with shorter timeouts or an explicit `jobs.idle_minutes` makes the idle stop come first. A worker that thinks silently for longer than the idle limit is stopped; raising `jobs.idle_minutes` is the fix.
